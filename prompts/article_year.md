## Required Article Structure (HTML format)

1. <h1> — engaging title
2. <p> Lead — 2-3 sentence summary
3. <h2> Highlights — <ul> with 5-7 key numbers
4. <h2> Activity Through the Year — seasonal patterns, peak months
5. <h2> Species Richness — total species, top families as <table>, notable records
6. <h2> Community — top observers as <table>, participation breadth
7. <h2> Geographic Patterns — if district data available, show as <table>
8. <h2> Comparison with Previous Year — changes, new species (if comparison data provided)
9. <h2> Notable New Species — if a list of new species is provided (species first recorded this year), write a short profile (2-4 sentences) for each notable one. Pick up to 10 most interesting species. For each:
   - Scientific name (italicized, linked to iNaturalist) and common name
   - Family and brief taxonomic context
   - Key biological traits: size, appearance, habitat, diet, behavior
   - Why it is notable (rare, endemic, indicator species, first record for region, etc.)
   - Use your knowledge of biology, but mark uncertain facts explicitly
10. <h2> Limitations — MUST include:
    - iNaturalist data reflect observer activity, not a complete biodiversity census
    - Lack of observations does not imply absence
    - Year-to-year changes may reflect effort, not ecology
    - Species profiles are based on general biological knowledge and may not reflect local Cyprus populations
11. <h2> Conclusion — brief forward-looking summary

Write in clean HTML. Use tables for data. Cite numbers from the provided data. Keep it engaging but scientifically responsible.
